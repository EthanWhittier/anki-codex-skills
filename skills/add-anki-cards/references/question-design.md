# Questions that preserve the learner's work

Use this guidance for newly authored study questions, card fronts, and generated review problems. Read it before designing the first prompt, then apply the brief check below before each presentation. Keep the check silent; it is not an extra learner approval or reporting step.

## Check the task before presenting it

1. Identify what the learner must supply: a term, ordering, method selection, explanation, diagnosis, or synthesis. Distinguish an independent attempt from guided teaching. A focused definition or recognition question is valid; do not make every question harder or add a justification requirement automatically.
2. Give the facts, assumptions, and constraints needed for a fair answer, while leaving the target work to the learner. If selecting a method is the target, do not name the method in a heading or supply a short menu unless recognition among supplied alternatives is intentional. If the target is explaining a known result, stating that result is appropriate. Necessary diagnostic evidence is not a forbidden hint.
3. Audit the complete learner-visible message, including headings, introductions, formatting, options, and any displayed learning objective. Could someone bypass the target knowledge by copying the wording, following the displayed order, matching aligned lists, noticing emphasis or grammatical cues, or merely repeating an announced conclusion? Revise unintended shortcuts. In particular:
   - For ordering tasks, scramble the items and verify that the displayed order is not already a correct answer. For matching, check that list positions or labels do not disclose the pairings.
   - Describe concrete constraints instead of announcing their interpretation when interpreting those constraints is the target. Avoid telling the learner a property is unusable and then asking which property is unusable.
   - When asking whether reasoning is sound, do not presuppose an error with "What is wrong?" unless locating an already-established error is the intended task. Similarly, do not state a trend or outcome that the learner is supposed to predict.
   - Check recent teaching for the same answer or reasoning path. Repeating a just-supplied explanation is rehearsal. For an independent application, use a changed case requiring a fresh decision, without a recap that solves it first; merely changing names is insufficient.
4. Establish a valid answer or acceptable range from the visible information and the learner's declared prerequisites. Check that a correct alternative would pass. Avoid "the best two" or a supposedly unique answer unless the stated goal and evidence support that ranking; otherwise ask for a justified choice and accept defensible alternatives. Removing clues must not make the problem vague, underspecified, or dependent on untaught knowledge.

## Teaching, hints, and repair

Start independent checks without embedded hints. Give a hint or direct explanation when requested or when teaching is needed; do not manufacture struggle. Treat resulting performance as assisted where the help supplies the target reasoning. Preserve the difference between selecting a method and successfully using a supplied method.

If a question has already leaked its answer, acknowledge the defect and do not count the leaked component as independent evidence or penalize the learner for the question's defect. Preserve any unaffected reasoning they demonstrated. When further evidence is useful, offer a fresh comparable case; do not immediately demand repetition of the exposed answer or impose a replacement quiz after a stop or skip.

During Anki review, preserve stored fixed prompts and frozen contracts. Do not silently rewrite a fixed card, alter an active problem, or change scheduling. Record a flawed stored prompt for an authorized edit under the review skill. For generated problems, repair or discard a flawed candidate before it is shown and frozen. After presentation, disclose defects and follow the existing ambiguity and user-rating rules.
