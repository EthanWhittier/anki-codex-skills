## 1. Stages

### Candidate

The learner has noticed a word or possible new sense. Keep unresolved candidates out of the review queue. Capture the encounter in conversation or a user-approved tracker until onboarding occurs.

### Recognition

Create one master sense note after the learner understands and approves the semantic packet. Train context-to-sense comprehension. Display the authoritative definition and grounding on the back.

Exit evidence:

- identify the selected sense in at least two sufficiently different contexts;
- recognize it in an actually presented spoken context when listening is a target;
- give a substantially correct working gloss;
- avoid the principal sibling-sense confusion.

### Precision

Train the definition's meaning-bearing components, boundaries, and closest contrast when this is valuable.

Exit evidence:

- explain the required components without relying on a circular synonym alone;
- distinguish one meaningful non-example or neighboring concept;
- recognize a borderline case as uncertain when appropriate.

### Activation

Train deliberate lexical access and appropriate use for learner-prioritized senses. Add the item to a small two-week cohort and create at most one initial active-use blueprint per sense.

Exit evidence:

- retrieve the target form from a constrained communicative intention;
- produce at least three natural uses across different contexts;
- use the verified grammatical frame and register;
- pronounce and inflect it adequately for the task when spoken use is a goal;
- complete at least one delayed prompt with the target word hidden.

### Integration

Train access during longer, less constrained expression. Use short speech, paragraph revision, explanation, or argument tasks where the target is useful but not visually supplied.

Exit evidence:

- use the item appropriately in a 30–60 second spoken or written response;
- retrieve it after a delay in a novel context;
- demonstrate that it improves precision rather than merely ornamenting the response.

### Maintenance

Use sparse, varied retrieval plus real encounters. Keep recognition and productive availability as separate metrics. Return to activation when the form no longer comes to mind or usage becomes unreliable.

### Retired

Use when an item is no longer valuable enough for active maintenance. Prefer suspension or demotion. Do not delete without explicit instruction.

## 2. Two-week activation cohort

During the first four weeks, recommend two sense-frames per cohort. After successful calibration, recommend three. Treat six to eight simultaneous active items as a caution threshold: explain the divided-practice cost before exceeding it, but honor an explicit learner override.

### Days 1–2: anchor

- revisit definition, source context, and boundaries;
- verify pronunciation, stress, and the useful inflected form when active speech is a goal;
- say two simple uses with the word visible;
- confirm the learner still prioritizes activation.

### Days 3–5: distinguish

- compare the main sibling sense or near-neighbor;
- complete one grammar-frame or collocation task;
- diagnose one non-example or misuse.

### Days 6–9: controlled production

- produce uses in two different scenarios;
- vary tense, subject, domain, or register only when natural;
- give feedback on meaning, syntax, collocation, and pragmatics separately.

### Days 10–12: lexical access

- hide the target word;
- cue the intended discourse move or meaning;
- record whether the target came to mind before revealing it;
- separate “a good alternative answer” from “retrieved the target.”

### Days 13–14: integration

- complete one 30–60 second spoken response or short paragraph;
- handle one unfamiliar generated context;
- revisit the required definition components after a delay;
- decide promote, extend activation, repair, or demote.

Do not schedule all activities as permanent cards. Use one generative blueprint plus transient guided practice when possible.

## 3. Promotion evidence

Treat promotion as an evidence judgment, not an automatic date change. Consider:

- Anki retrieval history and lapse pattern;
- performance on unfamiliar contexts;
- lexical access with the word hidden;
- natural collocation and grammatical frame;
- register judgment;
- user-reported or observed spontaneous use;
- confidence calibrated to actual performance.

Do not claim speech integration from typed answers alone. If audio is unavailable, ask the learner to speak first and honestly report or transcribe the response.

## 4. Repair and regression

Map the failure to one repair:

| Failure | Smallest repair |
|---|---|
| Wrong sibling sense | Pairwise contextual distinction |
| Definition missing one component | Focused precision prompt |
| Word recognized but not retrieved | Intent-to-word cue or cloze |
| Correct sense, unnatural phrase | Collocation or grammar-frame repair |
| Register mismatch | Audience/scenario comparison |
| Prompt admits too many answers | Rewrite master card |
| Definition itself disputed | Reopen source verification; freeze reviews until resolved |

After two repeated instances of the same diagnosed error, intervene even if Anki has not marked a leech. Regress one stage only when the earlier capability is actually unstable.

## 5. Audits and scaling

### Weekly

- prune or prioritize the deferred candidate queue;
- keep the active cohort within budget;
- inspect repeated error tags;
- notice real encounters and spontaneous uses;
- pause new activation when the cohort is not completing.

### Monthly

- sample mature recognition items in unseen contexts;
- sample active items with the target hidden;
- record a short unscripted speech or writing sample when the learner agrees;
- compare precision, naturalness, and access rather than raw rare-word counts;
- verify that stage labels match actual evidence and that retired candidates remain suspended;
- inspect vocabulary deck statistics before changing FSRS or intake rates.

### Long-term intake

Start with a soft budget of about seven recognition senses per week, allowing bursts of up to three per day, and two activated sense-frames per two-week cohort. After four healthy weeks, increase either recognition to about ten per week or activation to three per cohort. These are adjustable recommendations, not quotas or refusal conditions. Prefer high-utility distinctions, verbs, adjectives, collocations, and discourse frames over ornamental rarity.
