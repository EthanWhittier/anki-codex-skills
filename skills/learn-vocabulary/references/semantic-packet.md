# Semantic Packet

Use one packet per useful sense cluster. The packet preserves an authoritative definition while grounding it in the learner's understanding, situations, contrasts, and usage.

A dictionary definition is part of the record, not the whole understanding. Because verbal definitions ultimately depend on other words, ground the selected sense in observable cases, operations, contrasts, consequences, and boundary judgments. Do not pretend this eliminates every linguistic dependency; use the grounding layer to show what changes in the world or discourse when the word applies.

## Source and sense rules

- Prefer direct pages from established learner or general dictionaries such as Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, Merriam-Webster, Cambridge, or American Heritage.
- Store one short exact definition for the selected sense, the dictionary name, URL, sense number or label, and access date.
- Respect source quotation limits; do not reproduce full dictionary entries or long example collections.
- Keep the dictionary's wording distinct from the learner's working gloss.
- Use a second source when dictionaries divide the sense differently or when register, transitivity, or collocation is material.
- Do not manufacture a quotation or sense number. If the exact source cannot be verified, label the definition as a paraphrase.
- Select the sense licensed by the encounter. Record sibling senses without teaching them all at once.

## Required packet fields

### Identity

- **Lemma**: citation form.
- **Part of Speech**: include transitivity or construction when it distinguishes the sense.
- **Sense ID**: stable lowercase identifier, for example `qualify.v.statement`.
- **Pronunciation**: dialect-labeled when relevant.
- **Forms**: record spelling, stress, inflection, or a useful derivative only when it is irregular or material to recognition/use.

### Reference

- **Authoritative Definition**: short exact wording for this sense.
- **Definition Citation**: source, linked URL, sense label/number, access date.
- **Required Components**: the meaning-bearing conditions a correct explanation must contain.

### Learner model

- **Working Gloss**: concise wording supplied or explicitly approved by the learner.
- **Anchor Context**: the original encounter, or a clearly labeled curated example.
- **Source Encounter**: where the sentence or situation came from.

### Grounding

Choose only the useful forms:

- an observable scenario or transformation;
- one clear positive example;
- one non-example or boundary case;
- a contrast with the closest sense or neighboring word;
- consequences or inferences licensed by the word;
- an image, action, rule set, or decision procedure when available.

### Usage

- grammatical frame or argument structure;
- three to five high-value collocations or lexical bundles;
- register, connotation, domain, and dialect notes that affect appropriateness;
- a reusable spoken frame when active speech is a goal;
- a common misuse only when verified or observed.

When listening or pronunciation is a stated goal, use verified audio only when an authorized source or existing Anki media is available. Otherwise store dialect-labeled pronunciation guidance and state that auditory recognition has not yet been tested.

### Learning controls

- lifecycle stage;
- tags and stable link identifier;
- grader-required components and accepted equivalents;
- material confusions to reject;
- related senses, not automatically scheduled.

## Definition card policy

Every master recognition card displays the authoritative definition on the back. Add a separate precision-definition card only when exact conceptual articulation is high value or repeatedly weak.

Grade semantic components rather than verbatim overlap for ordinary vocabulary. Require verbatim wording only when the learner explicitly values quotation or when a stipulated technical definition depends on its phrasing.

## Quality audit

Before authoring, confirm:

- The selected sense fits the encounter.
- The definition is sourced and not silently paraphrased.
- The learner understands the concept rather than merely recognizing a synonym.
- The prompt tests one sense or one coherent distinction.
- The required components are visible in the prompt, back, or explicit task.
- Examples do not merely repeat the definition.
- The non-example differs for a meaningful reason.
- Usage claims are verified.
- Related senses will not create immediate interference.
- The source encounter is honestly labeled.

## Compact example

For `qualify.v.statement`:

- **Authoritative definition**: Oxford's statement-limiting sense.
- **Working gloss**: Add a limitation that makes a claim less sweeping.
- **Operation**: Replace an unrestricted conclusion with one limited to the studied population, measure, and duration.
- **Non-example**: Evidence that reverses the conclusion contradicts it rather than qualifying it.
- **Frame**: `qualify + claim/conclusion/statement`, often `by` or `with` a limitation.
- **Spoken frame**: “I'd qualify that by saying…”

Keep the exact sourced wording and citation in Anki rather than copying an unverified version from this example.
